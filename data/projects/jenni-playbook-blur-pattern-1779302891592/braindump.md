Jenni AI hit $10M ARR with 8 people, bootstrapped, by finding one niche (academic writing), building instant time-to-value (5 min to first draft), and nailing suspension-based conversion. Then declined because GPT wrappers have no moat.

The blur pattern: show the analysis free. Show the epic, architecture, timeline, and impl guide titles and section headers. Blur the content. The visitor sees the value exists but can't read it. That's the psychological trigger that converts. Jenni did this with citations — partially visible but blurred references. The itch to pay is psychological, not logical.

Conversion before virality: Jenni went from 1% to 2% to 3% to 4% conversion BEFORE investing in content marketing. Specview needs to measure the anonymous analyze to signup rate before doing Show HN. If below 3%, fix it before burning the channel.

Niche down ruthlessly: Jenni killed every marketing feature to go academic-only. Specview is engineering specs only. Not Life OS. Not business plans. Not weekly reviews. Engineering specs from messy thinking. That's it. Until $5K MRR.

The moat Jenni didn't have that Specview does: Jenni was inline autocomplete — a GPT wrapper anyone can copy. Specview is a multi-document pipeline (5 interconnected docs) with codebase context injection and a correction loop. That's architecture, not a feature. Copying one document is easy. Copying a pipeline where each doc builds on the previous with compounding context is hard.

Suspension-based pricing model: Free tier = analysis only (the hook). Pro tier = full spec suite (analysis + epic + architecture + timeline + impl guide). The analysis proves the product works. The blurred spec titles create the itch. The upgrade unlocks the rest.
