For Sam's situation (day job + finance domain + no audience + no time for mass outreach), Marc Lou's "sell before you build" pattern is the most relevant validation approach. Researched 2026-04-11 from multiple sources.

## The mechanic
1. **5-sentence personalized cold email max.** Ultra-personal opener, benefit-focused. Goal of the email is NOT a sale — it's a 30-min call.
2. On the call, **describe the concept verbally**, gauge interest, ask for a small **pre-pay deposit** (Marc Lou's first sale was $9 from an Australian escape-room owner).
3. If they say yes → build it. If they say no → ask why. If 30 emails get 0 yes → niche is wrong, not the tool.
4. After ~$4K MRR via cold outreach, switch to inbound (viral product features) for scale. Marc Lou eventually went from $4K MRR to $1M ARR this way.

## Objection handling
- Treat **"too expensive"** as "you didn't show me the ROI math". Respond with concrete 3–5x ROI calculation. Don't discount.
- Don't hard-sell. The pre-sell call is for validation, not closing.

## Success metrics (Sam-relevant calibration)
- 30 hyper-personalized emails to a tight ICP > 1000 generic emails
- Target: 1 reply that says "tell me more" per 10 emails = signal
- Target: 1 verbal "yes I'd pay €X/mo for this" per 30 emails = green light to build
- 0 yes in 30 = pivot niche, NOT pivot tool

## How to apply
Use this pattern (NOT high-volume CyberLeads-style outbound) when helping Sam draft cold outreach. He's optimizing for relevance per email, not throughput. The 5-sentence constraint forces him to actually use his finance insider language, which is his only real edge over other indie hackers.
