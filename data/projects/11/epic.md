# 🎯 Epic: ex-girlfriend

## Business Value

By next Wednesday, May 13, 2026, an ambiguous emotional question needs to become a structured, evidence-based decision: should a four-year relationship with an ex, paused for the last five months, restart? The stakes cut both ways. Saying "yes" to the wrong thing means months of the same conflict replaying itself. Saying "no" to the wrong thing means walking away from a relationship that may have genuinely matured during the time apart. The failure mode this epic is built to prevent is a decision shaped by anniversary symbolism or loneliness rather than evidence.

The "customer" in this work is Sam — sole decision-maker, sole beneficiary. The value delivered comes in three parts: clarity on whether the original cause of the breakup has actually shifted, an honest read on what the ex actually wants rather than what Sam hopes she wants, and a defensible answer ready for Wednesday's conversation that doesn't simply react to the date on the calendar.

There is no market here, no monetization, no second iteration. This is a one-shot personal decision against a hard deadline, and the deliverable is the decision itself together with the reasoning that produced it.

## Scope

### What This Epic Covers

The work begins with diagnosing the December 2025 breakup — sorting it into resolvable conflict, fundamental incompatibility, or external circumstance. From there, it assesses the five-month delta: what actually changed during the break, whether that change came from one party, both, or neither. Last Sunday's meeting gets a candid signal review, distinguishing mutual interest from one-sided interest from genuine ambiguity. The epic then moves into Wednesday conversation prep — the questions worth asking her, the positions worth holding, the signals worth watching for — and closes with an explicit go/no-go decision framework that produces a yes, a no, or an "extend the timeline" outcome before May 13.

### What This Epic Does NOT Cover

Long-term relationship structure — cohabitation, marriage, children — stays out of scope until at least one to two months of confirmed restart. Relitigating old fights belongs in the actual Wednesday conversation, not in this planning work. Anniversary symbolism is explicitly excluded as a decision input: May 13 is a date coincidence, not a signal, and if it ever becomes the sole reason to say yes, the right move is to defer. A unilateral "restart" declaration is also off the table — any restart requires her input, so what this epic produces is Sam's position, not the joint outcome.

## Success Criteria

By the time this epic closes, the original breakup cause should be categorized as resolvable conflict, incompatibility, or external circumstance, with a one-sentence justification attached. There should be a written answer to the question of what concretely changed across the five months, naming the person or people who changed and the specific behavior or circumstance involved. The Sunday meeting signal should be classified as mutual, one-sided, or ambiguous, without projecting onto her stated or unstated position. Three decision criteria should be written down before Wednesday: what makes it a yes, what makes it a no, and what triggers a thirty-day defer. The Wednesday conversation should go in armed with three to five written questions to ask her — questions, not statements to deliver. And by end of day on May 13, 2026, the decision should be made and the reason recorded — with the reason being something other than "because it's the anniversary."
