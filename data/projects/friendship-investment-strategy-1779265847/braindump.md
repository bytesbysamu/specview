# Friendship Investment Strategy

after the breakup i realized my social network is thin. too many couples friends, not enough independent friendships. my friend group has calcified — same 8 people rotating through the same activities. i need to expand, diversify, and deepen.

current state:
- ~8 close friends, mostly from uni and work
- 4 of them are in relationships, which means they're half-available
- social activities: dinner, drinks, poker night, occasionally sports
- i'm good at 1-on-1 deep conversations but bad at group social scenes
- i don't have a "crew" for spontaneous weekend plans
- zurich expat scene exists but feels transactional
- i speak german so i'm not limited to the english bubble

what i want in 12 months:
- 3-4 new genuine friendships (not just acquaintances)
- a regular group activity that creates organic bonding (sports team? climbing? music?)
- at least one friend i can call at 11pm when things are rough
- a social calendar where i never have a fully empty weekend
- friends across different contexts (not all tech people)

what i've tried:
- meetup.com events — felt forced, never converted to real friendships
- work colleagues — fine but limited, i don't want to depend on work for my social life
- dating apps — obviously not what i need right now

ideas i haven't tried:
- join a sports club/verein (very swiss, but this is how swiss people actually make friends)
- volunteering (genuine shared purpose, attracts good people)
- host something recurring at my place (dinner club? movie night?)
- take a class (cooking? language? dance?)
- become a regular at one bar/cafe instead of rotating

questions:
- what activities have the highest friendship-conversion rate?
- how do you go from "person i see at the activity" to "actual friend"?
- is it weird to strategize about friendship or is that just being intentional?
- should i focus on one expansion channel or try multiple?
- how do i balance depth (investing in existing friendships) vs breadth (meeting new people)?
- what's the role of my AI agent here? can it help track and prompt social actions?
